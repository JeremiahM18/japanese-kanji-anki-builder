# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

This project is a governed data pipeline and release-controlled content generation system for Japanese study decks. Collaboration must protect the accuracy of the repository, the people reviewing it, and the learners who may rely on its outputs.

## Expected Behavior

- Challenge claims with evidence from the current repository state, not memory or status guesses.
- Keep feedback specific, actionable, and tied to files, commands, data, or review criteria.
- Respect lane boundaries: source evidence, generated checks, human review, media identity, NLP support, and proof-ledger certification are separate authorities.
- Treat language, pedagogy, data quality, security, and release-readiness concerns as legitimate review blockers when evidence supports them.
- Disclose uncertainty plainly. If a fact cannot be verified, mark it as unverified rather than presenting it as complete.

## Unacceptable Behavior

- Harassment, threats, insults, sexualized comments, or discriminatory remarks.
- Dismissing review concerns without evidence.
- Pressuring maintainers or reviewers to lower certification, source-evidence, security, or release standards.
- Submitting or encouraging fabricated provenance, unverifiable proof, misleading documentation, or hidden generated-data changes.
- Publishing private information, credentials, local-only data, or third-party material without the right to distribute it.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns by opening a private security advisory or contacting the repository maintainer through the account listed in repository ownership metadata. Include:

- what happened
- where it happened
- who was involved
- links, screenshots, logs, or other evidence when safe to share
- whether there is an immediate safety, privacy, or credential risk

Do not include secrets, private learner data, or copyrighted source material in public issues.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may remove comments, close issues or pull requests, block contributors, revoke access, or report platform abuse. Enforcement decisions should be proportional to the conduct, the evidence available, and the risk to the project, reviewers, and learners.

Good-faith mistakes should be corrected with clear evidence and follow-up verification. Bad-faith behavior, repeated boundary violations, or attempts to weaken proof standards may result in removal from the project.
